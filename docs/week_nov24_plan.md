## 2. Part A: typos, style and length

### 2.1 Units [Frozen: `perturbations.jsonl`]

Built from the first turn of each of the 235 clean items.

| Kind | What changes | Units (valid) |
|---|---|---|
| `typo1`, `typo2`, `typo4` | 1, 2 or 4 character typos, each in a different word. Doses are nested: `typo2` contains `typo1`'s edit. | 235, 232, 128 |
| `slottypo1` | one typo inside a slot-value word ("itallian", "centtre") | 228 |
| `lower` | everything lower-cased | 233 |
| `nopunct` | sentence punctuation removed | 221 |
| `noapos` | apostrophes removed ("Im", "dont") | 76 |
| `fill1`, `fill2`, `fill4` | 1, 2 or 4 content-free sentences added, nested: thanks; greeting + thanks; greeting + lead-in + thanks + closing | 235 each |

**Typo rules:**
- A typo swaps two adjacent interior letters, deletes one, replaces one with a neighboring QWERTY key, or doubles one. The first and last letters stay.
- Only words of 4+ letters get typos. Protected words never do: slot values, slot words ("food", "price range", "area"), "no preference" phrases, and negations.
- A typo that spells a real word ("dine" → "die") is redrawn.
- Each typo1 op is used about equally: delete 64, double 58, neighbor 54, swap 59.

**Validity:** a unit is valid when the text changed and the regex slot extractor reads the same (food, area, pricerange) as before. `slottypo1` is the exception: it is valid by construction, and the extractor fails on 218 of its 228 units, as expected.

**Lengths:** median words added are 3, 8 and 18 for `fill1`, `fill2` and `fill4`, against a median turn of 13 words.

### 2.3 Scoring

- **Models:** the simulator study's saved runs: `qwen3_base`·F, `osim_mid`·S, `osim_final`·S, `userlm`·S, `qwen25_inst`·S, `qwen3_inst`·S.
- **What gets scored:** every valid unit under every clean probe text of its item. That is 20,637 pairs per model, plus `terse` and `fixed`.
- **Batching:** score each perturbed pair **in the same batch as its original**, and rescore the original there too. Every Δ is taken against the co-batched original, so batch-layout noise (0.15–0.5 nats in the simulator study) does not enter the comparison.
- **Samples:** typicality reuses each run's saved samples. No new sampling.
- Add a part `uperturb` to `sim_score.py` that reads `perturbations.jsonl` (and the A2/A3 units) the way `urewrite` reads `rewrites.jsonl`.

### 2.4 Metrics, per model and kind, with dialogue-bootstrap intervals

1. **Absolute cost:** median Δ log p(U | h) over the item's texts, per unit. For typos, also report it per typo and by op. For filler, report it against the number of added tokens.
2. **Inference effect:**
   - median |Δ log BF| over `bf_pairs`
   - the share of pairs where the Bayes factor changes sign
   - |Δ P(G)| and the posterior argmax flip rate over the item's hypothesis set
3. **Dose response:** the slope of |Δ log BF| and of Δ log p against the typo dose (1, 2, 4) and the filler dose. Fit by least squares on unit means, with a bootstrap interval.
4. **Slot typos:** the share of the named slot's Bayes factor kept, for (G, drop:X) where X is the slot the typo hit: log BF after ÷ log BF before, median over units where log BF before > 1.
5. **Typicality:** for `glob1A`, `glob1B`, `cent1B`, `cent1B:6bins` and the Part C variants, report:
   - the share of human pairs whose bin changes
   - the share moved into the extreme bin
   - |Δ score|

## 4. Part C: quantile bins (CPU, saved samples)

New variants in `configs/typicality_v0.json`, under `"quantile"`:

| Variant | Statistic | Bins |
|---|---|---|
| `glob1Q` | S | one-sided, at the pooled-sample quantiles 0.50, 0.70, 0.85, 0.95, 0.99 of global T |
| `cent1Q` | cent | the same |

- **Bin masses:** among the reference's own samples, the bins hold 50 / 20 / 15 / 10 / 4 / 1%.
- **Pool:** the 463 distinct clean hypothesis texts, uniform weights, exactly as `glob` and `cent` use.
- **Score (option B form):** −(H + σ·c_b), where c_b is the mean T of the pooled samples in bin b. In the top bin, c_b is the pooled 0.995 quantile of T, so a human turn beyond all samples gets a capped score.
- **Sensitivity:** quantiles 0.50, 0.80, 0.90.
- Evaluate every model on the simulator study's frozen metrics:
  - share of human pairs in the extreme bin
  - size principle kept
  - contradiction log Bayes factor
  - bin agreement under `para1`, `typo`, `pad`, `verbose`, `zh`
  - then the same under Part A's kinds and Part B's hypothesis rewordings

## 5. Part D: a first trained typicality estimator

### 5.1 Teacher and target [Frozen at STOP 3]

- **Teacher:** typicality with `qwen3_base`·F as reference, i.e. the few-shot base model's centered statistic s(x | h) = S(x | h) − S(x | broad|impl), normalized globally: T = (s − H)/σ. With global normalization, T is a linear function of −log BF(h vs broad). The student is therefore learning a binned likelihood ratio.
- **Regression target:** T itself, clipped to the pooled 0.5% and 99.5% quantiles.
- **Classification target:** the bin of T under the bin scheme chosen at STOP 3. The choice is between `cent1B:6bins` and `cent1Q`, by this rule:
  - Use `cent1Q` if, with `qwen3_base` as reference, its bin agreement under both `para1` and `typo1` is within 2 points of `cent1B:6bins` or better, and its size principle kept is between 0.5 and 2.
  - Otherwise use `cent1B:6bins`.

### 5.2 Training data (synthetic only; no human turn is ever trained on)

- **Matched pairs:** each of the 64 saved samples x of each of the 463 texts h, with its own S(x | h) and S(x | broad). That is 29,632 pairs.
- **Cross pairs:** within each item, the first 32 samples of each text h, scored under every other text h′ of the same item.
  - There are 9,084 distinct (h, h′) text pairs, so 290,688 pairs before the split. One new GPU job (`xscore`) scores S(x | h′) with `qwen3_base`·F.
  - Cross pairs teach the student that "Italian" is atypical under a Chinese goal and under a goal that leaves food open.
- **Exclusions:** samples that are cut off (no end marker) or empty. Report their share.

### 5.3 Split [Frozen]

- Draw 20% of the food values that occur in clean gold goals, seeded. These are the held-out foods.
- Remove from training every text that mentions a held-out food, and every sample whose regex slot tuple names one.
- Hold out 5% of the remaining training samples (by sample, so a sample's matched and cross pairs stay together) for validation.
- Evaluate on all 235 human turns, reported separately for **seen-food** items and **unseen-food** items (gold goal names a held-out food).

### 5.4 Student

- **Backbone:** `Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B-Base`, pinned revision, LoRA r = 16 on every linear layer.
- **Input:** `Customer description: {h}\nCustomer message: {x}` plus the end token. The head reads the last token's final hidden state.
- **Runs:**

  | Run | Head | Loss |
  |---|---|---|
  | `R-lin` | linear | MSE on T |
  | `R-mlp` | MLP, one hidden layer of 512, GELU | MSE on T |
  | `C-mlp` | MLP as above | cross-entropy on the bins |

- **Training settings:**
  - learning rates: 2e-4 for LoRA, 1e-3 for the head; cosine schedule
  - batch 64, 2 epochs, bf16
  - keep the checkpoint with the best validation loss
- **Recorded:** training time and pairs per second at evaluation.
- **Turning predictions into scores:**
  - A regression student's T̂ becomes a bin score (−(H + σ·c_b) of its bin) and a continuous score −(H + σ·T̂).
  - A classification student's output becomes the score of its argmax bin, and the expected score Σ_b p_b·(−(H + σ·c_b)).

### 5.5 Baselines on the same human pairs

1. Teacher typicality (the target, computed from 64 samples per text with an 8B model).
2. Raw log-likelihood of `qwen3_base`·F: the 8B reference model.
3. Raw log-likelihood of `Qwen3-0.6B-Base` with the same F prompt, zero-shot. This answers whether a small model's own log-prob is already enough.
4. Jev, from the existing MultiWOZ run, where its pairs match.

### 5.6 Evaluation (human pairs; held-out-food items reported separately)

1. **Agreement with the teacher:** Spearman of T̂ against T; exact bin agreement; agreement within one bin.
2. **Inference, with the simulator study's frozen metrics:**
   - size principle kept (implicit, slot named and slot omitted)
   - contradiction log Bayes factor
   - mean log P(G) and top-1 accuracy over the item's hypothesis set
3. **Robustness:** bin agreement, |Δ score| and argmax flips under:
   - the utterance rewrites (`para1`, `typo`, `pad`, `verbose`, `zh`)
   - Part A's kinds
   - hypothesis rewording. The student scores a reworded hypothesis directly, with no new samples, which the teacher cannot do.
4. **Cost:** pairs per second for the student, against the teacher's samples plus scoring for one new hypothesis.

### 5.7 Predictions and verdict

| ID | Prediction |
|---|---|
| D1 | The student learns the teacher: Spearman ≥ 0.7 between T̂ and T on human pairs, for at least one run. |
| D2 | It keeps the size principle: size principle kept between 0.5 and 2 for slot named. |
| D3 | It keeps contradiction: contradiction log Bayes factor ≥ 3 nats. |
| D4 | It is at least as robust as its teacher: bin agreement under `para1` ≥ the teacher's (86.4% for `cent1B` with `qwen3_base`). |
| D5 | It is cheap: ≥ 100 times more pairs per second than the teacher for a new hypothesis text. |
| D6 | The small model's own log-prob (baseline 3) is worse than the student on both the size principle kept (\|ratio − 1\|) and \|Δ log BF\| under `para1`. |
| D7 | Unseen foods cost little: D1's Spearman drops by ≤ 0.1 on unseen-food items. |

**Verdict [Frozen]:**
- "The pipeline works" if D1–D3 hold for at least one run.
- "Learns the teacher but not the task" if D1 holds and D2 or D3 fails.
- Otherwise "does not learn the teacher". Name the likeliest cause: the shift from synthetic to human text, the target's noise, or capacity. Show the evidence for it: student-vs-teacher agreement on held-out synthetic pairs against human pairs.
